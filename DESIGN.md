# DESIGN.md: karankapur.com, v3 "Fork"

This is the design contract for the site. Code that disagrees with this file is wrong, not the file. Changes to this file need an entry in the brief's DECISIONS.md. The v2 branch holds the previous contract ("Crate").

## 1. Visual theme and atmosphere

A dark room with one lamp on. The whole page is a single warm near-black sheet, cut into sections by hairlines, with a lot of air and a small number of things that move. The recurring object is a session that forks: the hero is a real 3D lattice of KV cache blocks that forks into four branches, the thaw entry under Experience opens into a scroll-driven camera tour through what the storage layer saves, and the favicon is the same mark. Each project card carries its own small procedural 3D scene, and the sound control is a turntable. Every Experience and Leadership entry carries a small scene of its own, a tile beside its text, drawn in the same hand. The accent is ember, used like a highlighter: fork points, the one button, metric values, the live token, the record label. Nothing glows, nothing blurs, nothing is a gradient. The 3D is flat shaded with hairline edges, like an engineering drawing that happens to move. It should feel like a terminal someone with taste set up, not a product landing page.

The 3D references are Vektr (near-black hero, one accent, a draggable object, readouts at the sides in mono, a scroll-driven engineering tour) and a photographer template's tilted cards floating in perspective. Every piece of geometry is procedural and built in code: no downloaded models, no stock images, no textures.

Audience: a recruiter skimming for 60 seconds, then a senior engineer opening DevTools. The hero names the target roles in the first screen. Every number has its hardware next to it and a receipt link.

## 3. Typography rules

- Display and body: Bricolage Grotesque, variable (opsz 12 to 96, wdth 75 to 100, wght 200 to 800), one self-hosted latin woff2 in `src/assets/fonts`, `font-display: swap`, preloaded from the page head (the stylesheet is inlined, so the preload is how the browser learns of the font before layout).
- Labels, dates, captions, diagram text: DM Mono 400 and 500, self-hosted from `@fontsource/dm-mono`.
- Fallbacks: a size-adjusted `Bricolage Fallback` over Helvetica Neue or Arial, and `DM Mono Fallback` over Menlo, so first paint and swapped paint line up.
- The pairing's character is width: display type is condensed (`wdth 75` to `80`) at large optical size, body is full width at `opsz 14`. H1 `clamp(3.6rem, min(11vw, 12vh), 9rem)` (the height term keeps the first screen whole on a 1440 by 900 desktop), line-height 0.88, tracking -0.035em, `wdth 75`. H2 `clamp(2.4rem, 6vw, 4.6rem)`, line-height 0.95, `wdth 80`. Project H3 `clamp(1.5rem, 2.4vw, 1.9rem)`, `wdth 78`. The featured role line `clamp(1.4rem, 2.6vw, 2rem)`, `wdth 85`. Contact H2 up to 11rem, `wdth 75`.
- Body 1.05rem/1.55. Mono labels 0.8rem, sentence case, never uppercase. Metric values are mono 500, `tabular-nums`, the unit a `<small>` at 0.55em.
- Every H2 ends in a period, and the period is Ember (`<span class="dot">`). Headlines are plain statements. Sentence case. No puns, no "elevate", no em dashes anywhere in copy or code comments.
- The hero intro is approved wording and is stored in `src/content/profile/main.json`. Do not paraphrase it.

## 5. Layout principles

- One Ink sheet. Content sits in a centered column, `max-width: 1180px`, `padding-inline: var(--gut)` where `--gut` is `clamp(1rem, 4vw, 3rem)`. Sections are separated by a single hairline, never by a color change.
- Every section after the hero is a two-column grid: an 11rem left rail holding the sticky mono label (and, in Experience, the degree line), then the content. Inside Experience and Leadership each row is its own grid: a 16rem tile column (14rem at 820px and below), the text, the dates; the sentence spans the text and date columns, not the tile's. The hero is its own grid, 1.1fr/0.9fr, text left, graphic right, `min-height: 100vh` minus the header; inside the copy, the name and the tagline share a grid with the headshot in a second column. The tour sits inside the Experience content column, so its stage is the column's width (it pulls back past the tile column); at 820px and below the rail stacks and the stage is the full width.
- Reading measures: intro 46ch, thaw summary 58ch, project copy 42ch, card caption 36ch, role lines 60ch (64ch on the featured entry).
- Section vertical padding `clamp(3rem, 7vw, 6rem)`; contact `clamp(4rem, 10vw, 8rem)`.
- Page order is fixed: header, hero with intro and controls, experience (thaw first and featured, then the other roles), projects, leadership (when it has entries), contact, footer. thaw is not a section of its own and does not lead.

## 6. Depth and elevation

Flat. There is no elevation system. Hierarchy comes from type size, Bone versus Bone 2 versus Mute, and hairlines. Ink 2 is a fill for small boxes and the cards, not a surface system. Hover states move things (translate, scale, rotate) or change a color; they never add a shadow, a glow, a blur or a border thickness.

## 7. Motion

Motion has a job or it is not here. CSS motion is compositor-only (transform, opacity). The WebGL scenes run on their own canvases and follow the same rules written out in section 10. The page script stays small: it toggles classes, runs the sound control, and imports a scene chunk when its canvas nears the viewport.

1. **Hero rise.** The two H1 lines, tagline, intro, the buttons row and the three HUD blocks rise 0.5rem, 90 ms apart. The name and the intro only move; they are the largest text on the page and stay visible from the first paint, so the largest contentful paint is the first paint. The tagline, buttons and HUD also fade in. Sets reading order without hiding the name.
2. **Hero fork.** The lattice forks when you drag sideways, scroll the first 60% of a viewport, or press "Fork it": the four branches fly apart along quadratic curves on a damped spring (a touch of overshoot, then settle), the new token columns grow in one after another, the branch ends drift by a few hundredths of a unit once settled. The site's thesis, done by the visitor.
3. **Hero orbit and parallax.** Drag to orbit within the limits, release for inertia that decays in about a second and eases back inside the limits; the pointer position nudges the rig and camera by a few degrees. The object floats 0.06 units on a slow sine. An Ember head steps along the trunk three columns a second: the session is running.
4. **Fork field.** The hero's background is one 64px tile in Line 2, a short token tick and a tiny fork mark, repeated as a flat hairline pattern on one composited layer that drifts one tile diagonally every 18 s (`translate` only, so the compositor moves it and the main thread does nothing: under 2% CPU). Paused when the hero is off screen or the tab is hidden (the page script sets `.paused`); off under reduced motion. No gradient, no glow, no second layer.
5. **Turntable.** While the player reports playing: the arm lowers and swings in (damped, about a second), the record spins up to 33 and a third over about two seconds and the arm tracks inward across a ninety second side, seven bars rise and fall on two summed sines each. Paused: the arm lifts, parks, the record spins down, the bars settle to a rest height, and the scene stops drawing once nothing moves. Says "playing" with the object instead of a badge, and only when it is true: the state comes from the widget's play and pause events, not from the press. The caption's three bars (`scaleY` 0.25 to 1) and, before the scene loads, the SVG disc (`rotate`) and its five bars (`scaleY`) do the same job by CSS; all pause with the hero.
6. **Scroll reveals.** Headings, copy and rows (`.rv`) rise 1.5rem on a `view()` timeline over the first 35% of entry. Experience markers scale in on the same timeline.
7. **Tour camera.** Inside the open disclosure, native scrolling drives the camera through the five views: position and target lerp between keyframes with a smoothstep and a small lift mid-move, a damped follow smooths the scroll input, the subsystem in view fades its hairlines from Ink to Ember, and in the last view three copies of the snapshot travel out along Ember curves that draw as they go. Captions cross-fade and rise 0.6rem. No scroll hijacking: the page scrolls as usual, the stage is merely sticky. Opening or closing the disclosure recomputes the progress; the disclosure's caret turns 90 degrees.
8. **Card drift, tilt, lift.** Each card drifts on three slow sines (12, 9 and 16 px), tilts up to 10 degrees so the edge nearest the pointer comes forward, and lifts 70px on hover or focus; its wire frame follows exactly and turns Ember. The floor slides a little with the pointer. The cards are HTML, so the text stays crisp and every link is a real link. The scene inside each card rides along.
9. **Project scenes.** Every position in the gate, the agency and the field is a pure function of time (see section 4), so a scene can start at any instant, nothing accumulates, and the reduced-motion still is one instant of the same motion. Each scene yaws by 0.04 rad on a 20 to 30 second sine so it is never frozen.
10. **Links.** Header links grow an Ember underline; contact links fill theirs; the Contact button lifts 2px; the card arrow nudges diagonally.
11. **Tiles.** Each entry's tile drifts on two slow sines (4 and 3 px), tilts up to 8 degrees so the edge nearest the pointer comes forward while the pointer is within a tile's width of it, and lifts 28px on hover; the hairline turns Ember while lifted. Transform only, on the inner box; the scene rides along. Each tile runs only while on screen and the tab is visible. The role scenes inside follow rule 9: every position a pure function of time.

Engines without scroll-driven animations get the finished state: every scroll keyframe defines only `from`, so the base state is the end state. `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` collapses every duration, removes the hero entrance, stops the field, the bars and the disc, drops every scroll animation, lays the tour out as a still frame with its five captions listed under it, leaves the tiles where CSS put them (the tile module is not loaded), and makes each scene render exactly one frame (the hero forked and settled, the tour at the fork view, the cards in their resting slots, each project scene and each role scene at one instant, the turntable parked or, when sound is on, with the arm down and the bars at fixed heights) with no loop and no pointer handling. Nothing is half drawn in that mode.

Banned: count-ups, typing effects, cursor followers, parallax on text, scroll hijacking, glows, bloom, halos, gradient fills as decoration, blurs, shadows, autoplaying audio or video, purple. Hover tilt is allowed on the project deck and the entry tiles only, where it is the point.

## 8. Responsive behavior

## 9. Agent prompt guide

When asked to change the site:

- Read this file, then `CLAUDE.md`, then the content in `src/content`. Numbers come from `_brief/RECEIPTS.md` only; a metric without a `source` URL will not build and should not exist. The HUD's "about 340 s cold boot" comparison is the one figure shown by owner request before its receipt is on thaw.sh; it is linked to the fork receipt JSON and must go if that link stops holding it.
- Add copy in sentence case with periods. No em dashes, no emoji, no numbered eyebrows, no uppercase labels, no "elevate" or "seamless", no puns.
- Use only the nine colors in section 2 and the two typefaces in section 3. No new radii, shadows, gradients, blurs or fonts.
- Keep the page order in section 5. thaw stays inside Experience as its first entry; Leadership renders only from `src/content/leadership` and is never hand-written.
- Sound stays off until pressed; no third-party iframe or script may exist in the DOM on load. After the first press there is exactly one iframe, the SoundCloud widget, hidden and inert, for the rest of the visit. The tracks and their order are in `src/content/profile/main.json` and come from DECISIONS.md; the now-playing line always names the track and artist and links to the track on SoundCloud. The turntable and its bars are decoration: never describe them as reacting to the music.
- Every animation must have a one-line job in section 7, use transform, opacity or clip-path, pause when off screen if it loops, and be covered by the reduced-motion block with a finished static state.
- 3D work follows section 10. Geometry is procedural and lives in `src/scenes`; never add a model file or a texture image. A new card scene exports `mount(SceneOptions)` and is registered in the page script's loader map by project id. A new Experience or Leadership entry needs a scene in `src/scenes/roles.ts` and a drawing in `Mark.astro`, both keyed by the entry's file name without its order prefix; an entry without one keeps an empty tile, so do not ship that.
- Run `npm run check`, `npm run build`, `npm run test:e2e` and `npm run lhci` before calling work done. The budgets in `lighthouserc.cjs` are the exit criteria. The script budget there covers the one page script; the three.js chunks load only on hardware WebGL, so the Lighthouse run is pinned to a software rasterizer (`--disable-gpu --use-angle=swiftshader`), the same condition CI without a GPU and the Playwright run have, and they sit outside that run (see section 10). The new headless mode on a Mac with a GPU would otherwise find hardware WebGL and fetch the hero scene after idle.

## 10. WebGL rules

- One library, three.js, imported with a dynamic `import()` from `src/scenes/*.ts` only when the scene's canvas comes within 320px of the viewport (and, for the hero and the turntable, after an idle callback so they never compete with first paint). Eight scene chunks (hero, tour, turntable, deck backdrop, gate, agency, field, and the role scenes in one chunk) share one three.js chunk. The two motion modules (cards, tiles) hold no three.js and also load on approach. The page loads exactly one script of its own, `src/scripts/page.ts`, bundled to a single module of a few KB with no preload helper; nothing else is in the first load.
- One WebGL probe per page, in `hasWebGL()`, taken lazily by the page script the first time a stage asks for its scene (the hero, after idle), never during the script's own evaluation before the first paint. A software rasterizer (SwiftShader, llvmpipe, the Windows basic driver) counts as no WebGL: it would draw these scenes at a few frames a second while pinning a CPU core, and the static drawings are the better page. Headless Chrome without a GPU, which is what CI, Playwright and the Lighthouse run (pinned there by its Chrome flags) have, takes this path.
- Device pixel ratio capped at 2, or 1.5 on `pointer: coarse`.
- Every loop runs through `Loop` in `src/scenes/gl.ts`: it stops when the stage leaves the viewport (IntersectionObserver) or the tab is hidden, and dt is clamped so nothing jumps on return. The tour renders only while the camera is still moving; the turntable stops drawing once it is parked and still. The small scenes (the project cards, the role tiles, the turntable) share the `Stage` shell in `gl.ts` (renderer, lights, fixed orbit camera refit on resize, the loop, one still frame under reduced motion).
- Every mount returns a handle with `dispose()`, called on `pagehide`: geometries, materials, the renderer.
- Materials: Lambert faces with an ambient plus one key and one fill light, so a block reads as three flat tones; a hairline `LineSegments` edge set in Ink on every box, rebuilt from the instance matrices. The record and its label are the only non-box meshes, flat cylinders with hairline rims. No bloom, no halos, no fog, no transparency, no gradients, no textures. Colors are the nine palette hexes.
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`: one frame, no loop, no pointer handlers. The turntable redraws one frame when the sound state changes.
- No WebGL: the static SVG in each stage stays (`.stage.is-3d` is never set), the cards still lay out and move with the small CSS-only motion module, and the tiles still drift, tilt and lift with theirs.
- Nothing a keyboard user needs is on a canvas. Canvases are `aria-hidden`; the HUD, the fork control, the play control, the disclosure, the captions and every card link are HTML.
